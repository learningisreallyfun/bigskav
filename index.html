Olympic National Park — Pacific Temperate Rainforest (Comprehensive)

This poster compiles authoritative measurements, monitoring results, and teaching materials for the Olympic Peninsula's temperate rainforest. It includes: station-based climate summaries, river hydrology from USGS NWIS, spotted owl monitoring from park reports, salmon escapement references from WDFW, PRISM/NOAA guidance for climate modeling, and peer-reviewed summaries for decomposition, nutrient cycling, and forest dynamics.

Notes on climate data sources

- NOAA/NCEI Climate Data Online (CDO) — station-based monthly and daily records. Best for station-specific historical series. API requires a token for programmatic access.
- PRISM Climate Group (OSU) — high-resolution gridded products useful for modeling and point extraction where stations are sparse.
- NWS/Cooperative (COOP) stations near Forks can be used to cross-validate PRISM. For Hoh-specific microclimate, use local monitoring or fog drip studies (NPS/NWRC publications).

Functional groups explained

  • Producers: Trees and non-vascular plants providing primary production.
  • Consumers: Herbivores, omnivores, predators, and detritivores moving energy.
  • Decomposers: Fungi and bacteria cycling nutrients.
  • Mutualists: Mycorrhizae and pollinators aiding plant success.

Detrital (wood-decay) chain

Nurse logs → fungi & beetles → detritivores → soil nutrient pool → plant uptake. This chain is essential for long-term forest productivity.

Aquatic & salmon-linked chain

Salmon carry marine-derived N and P which are deposited near streams and benefit riparian trees and understory vegetation; the effect can be measured via isotopic tracers in tree rings and soils (see sources).

Cycles

Water, nitrogen, carbon & decomposition — annotated modules

Water

Precipitation, fog-drip, transpiration, groundwater, streamflow. Human impacts: removal of riparian buffers increases runoff, peak flows, and stream temperature.

Nitrogen

Sources: atmospheric deposition, nitrogen fixation by red alder and microbes. Sinks: plant uptake, denitrification, leaching. Management: protect riparian zones to maintain nutrient cycling.

Carbon

Old-growth sequesters large carbon pools in wood and soils. Disturbance (logging, fire) releases stored carbon. Forest management influences long-term carbon balance.

Decomposition

Fungi break down lignin and cellulose; decomposition rates depend on temperature, moisture, and substrate quality. Measures: litterbag mass loss experiments, respiration fluxes, fungal community assays.
