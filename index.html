Deep dive

Abiotic factors — detailed explanations, mechanisms, and measurement

For each factor we include how it is measured, why it matters, expected spatial patterns, and likely responses to climate change.

Precipitation & fog

Measurement: weather stations (rain gauges), fog collectors, remote sensing. Mechanism: orographic lift and coastal moisture advection. Response: changes in storm tracks change total and seasonal distribution; fog decline reduces summer water inputs.

Temperature & microclimate

Measurement: paired sensors (canopy vs understory), stream thermographs. Mechanism: canopy buffering moderates extremes; loss of canopy increases diurnal range. Response: warmer springs change phenology and salmon timing.

Soils & nurse logs

Soil pits & organic horizon measurements reveal deep carbon pools; nurse logs provide substrate and nutrients for seedlings — preserving downed wood is essential for long-term forest dynamics.

Hydrology & stream temperature

Watershed monitoring, stream gauges, and thermal networks reveal how logging and riparian removal increase stream temperature and reduce salmon survival.

Quick glossary
Net primary production — carbon fixed by plants minus plant respiration.
Trophic cascade — indirect interactions where predators regulate lower trophic levels.
Nurse log — fallen log providing substrate for seedlings and nutrient pulses.
Mycorrhiza — symbiotic fungus-root association that aids nutrient uptake.
