Abiotic factors — extensive listing with roles

Physical and chemical conditions that shape species distributions, processes, and rates.

Climate & Hydrology

  • Precipitation patterns: Rain shadows, orographic lifting, very high coastal totals.
  • Fog & cloud immersion: Critical to summer water balance and epiphyte hydration.
  • Streamflow & hydrographs: Seasonal peaks control salmon migration and spawning habitat.
  • Groundwater & wetlands: Provide recharge and refugia for amphibians and invertebrates.

Substrate & chemistry

  • Nutrient pools: Organic horizons, nurse logs and mycorrhizal networks store and redistribute N and P.
  • Mineralogy: Bedrock weathering influences base cations and pH.
  • Temperature buffering: Ocean proximity and canopy moderates extremes.

Disturbance & physical processes

  • Windthrow & gap dynamics — generate microsites for regeneration and create structural diversity.
  • Mass wasting — landslides expose mineral soil and reset successional trajectories.
  • Sea-level & coastline change — reshape estuarine habitats over centuries.

Anthropogenic modifiers

  • Logging & fragmentation — reduce old-growth, increase edge effects, and alter hydrology.
  • Roads & barriers — interfere with animal movement and salmon access.
  • Climate change — shifts in precipitation form, ocean productivity, phenology timing, and disturbance regimes.

Biotic factors — long, annotated list

Producers, consumers, decomposers, mutualists, pathogens, and functional groups with short role descriptions.

Producers

  • Sitka spruce — canopy-forming, high biomass.
  • Western hemlock — shade-tolerant, understory recruitment.
  • Western redcedar — structure & habitat.
  • Sword fern — widespread groundcover.
  • Red alder — nitrogen fixer on disturbed sites.
  • Epiphytes & moss mats — large biomass reservoirs on trunks/branches.

Primary consumers & detritivores

  • Roosevelt elk, Black-tailed deer — bulk herbivores shaping regeneration.
  • Banana slug — detritivore linking litter to microbial food web.
  • Insect herbivores — leaf and stem feeders, important in nutrient cycling.

Secondary & apex consumers

  • Bobcat, Cougar — control meso- and ungulate populations.
  • Northern spotted owl — old-growth specialist.
  • Black bear & bald eagle — important salmon predators and nutrient redistributors.

Decomposers, microbes & fungi

  • Wood-decay fungi — primary wood decomposition agents.
  • Mycorrhizae — improve plant nutrient uptake and connect trees to networks.
  • Soil bacteria — N mineralization, nitrification, denitrification.

Mutualists & commensals

  • Lichens — symbiosis with algae/cyanobacteria.
  • Epiphytic flora — use trees for substrate; provide microhabitats.
  • Cleaner organisms & pollinators — insects and birds performing ecosystem services.

Pathogens & parasites

  • Ticks — parasitize mammals.
  • Root pathogens — can create differential mortality and open niches.
  • Introduced species — some insects or plants change competition outcomes.

Cycles

Water, nitrogen, carbon — animated and annotated

Animated arrows show flux direction; click boxes for details, and view how human actions change flows.

Water cycle — rainforest
Precipitation → interception → transpiration → runoff → evaporation. Forests increase interception and regulate streamflow.
Nitrogen cycle
Fixation by alder & bacteria → plant uptake → herbivores → decomposition → denitrification or recycled nitrogen.
Carbon cycle
Photosynthesis draws CO₂ → biomass & wood; respiration & decay release CO₂; old-growth stores carbon long-term.
